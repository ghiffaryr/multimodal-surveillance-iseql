\chapter{Discussion}
\label{Discussion}

This chapter interprets the evaluation results (Section~\ref{sec:disc-interp}), analyses the role of union in multimodal design (Section~\ref{sec:disc-union}), acknowledges threats to validity (Section~\ref{sec:disc-threats}), and summarises the contributions (Section~\ref{sec:disc-contrib}).

\section{Interpretation of Results}
\label{sec:disc-interp}

\textbf{RQ1: Object re-identification.} The effect is concentrated on the events that require a persistent object identity over time. Without re-ID, per-frame ID assignment fragments intervals into single frames, so loitering, vehicle escape, and handoff queries rarely fire. For Gemini 3.6 Flash, re-ID raises loitering from 0/5 to 5/5, vehicle escape from 0/5 to 1/5, and handoff from 4/5 to 5/5, lifting visual F1 from 0.706 to 0.847. The improvement is consistent across all four VLM models for these tracking-dependent events, spanning two different architectures (Mistral-based Pixtral/Ministral and Google-based Gemini). The mechanism is simple, two-phase prompt engineering with block-overlap matching, yet sufficient to recover the tracking-dependent events. No separate tracking model or training data is required, which is important for reproducibility and for application to future VLM architectures. Notably, Pixtral 12B shows slightly more detections overall without re-ID (21 vs.\ 19 TP), confirming that re-ID is important specifically for the duration-gated events rather than for all detection. A caveat applies to handoff: with the deterministic temperature $=0$, seed $=42$ setup, the object ID is sometimes reused across frames even when the person ID is not, and since the query matches two different person IDs carrying the same object ID, Ministral and Pixtral already detect 5/5 handoff scenes without re-ID. Without determinism (as in VIS MODE/GATCHA), even the object ID churns on every call, so the handoff query cannot match and fails completely. The re-ID benefit for handoff is therefore smaller than for loitering and vehicle escape, which require a persistent person identity.

\textbf{RQ2: Audio model ablation.} Qwen2-Audio achieves perfect precision (1.000) for all window configurations of 2.5 s or larger. Every detection is correct. This is a critical property for the multimodal union: false positives from audio would propagate to condition C and degrade its precision. The LALM's precision is not matched by PANNs CNN14, whose highest F1 (0.429) is less than 60\% of the LALM's best micro F1 (0.788). The best Qwen2-Audio configuration is 5.0 s / 2.5 s (F1 = 0.788), with 2.5 s / 1.25 s close behind (F1 = 0.750); this balances acoustic context with temporal resolution for both providers.

The per-event analysis reveals distinct model strengths. Qwen2-Audio is strongest on vehicle escape (perfect recall, 5/5) and gunshot (5/5), but fails on fight detection entirely (0/5). The fight failure is instructive: the shout-to-impact temporal pattern may be too subtle or too tightly spaced for the 2.5 s window to capture both sounds as distinct events, or the fight sounds in the synthetic dataset may lack sufficient acoustic distinctiveness. PANNs achieves its best performance on gunshot (5/5) but with lower precision due to false positives, and fails entirely on fight and vehicle escape. This asymmetry suggests that the two models could be complementary in a hierarchical detection design.

\textbf{RQ3: Multimodal vs.\ unimodal.} The union-based condition C achieves 30/30 TP (F1 = 0.938, 4 FP, 0 FN) with Gemini 3.6 Flash + Qwen2-Audio, outperforming both visual-only (best F1 = 0.847) and audio-only (best micro F1 = 0.788). The thesis claim $C \ge \max(A, B)$ holds on every scene.

\section{The Role of Union in Multimodal Design}
\label{sec:disc-union}

The design choice of union over temporal cross-modal join merits discussion. The conventional intuition is that multimodal fusion should require coincidence: an audio event is confirmed by a temporally proximate visual event, and vice versa. However, this approach has a fundamental asymmetry in forensic applications. The cost of a false negative (a missed event) far exceeds the cost of a false positive (an unnecessary operator review), because a missed event could mean a missed crime. Temporal join reduces false positives at the expense of increased false negatives ,  the opposite of what a forensic system should optimise for.

Union makes the opposite trade-off: it preserves every detection from every modality, at the cost of potentially more false positives. But the union approach is auditable: every detection carries a modality tag, so an operator reviewing the output can see whether a detection came from audio, visual, or both. The experiment demonstrates that union is the correct operation for forensic surveillance: $C$ consistently dominates $\max(A, B)$ with no exceptions.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:disc-threats}

\textbf{Internal validity.} The evaluation uses synthetic videos generated by Dreamina Seedance. Synthetic data allows precise control over event timing and modality signal, but may not capture the variability of real CCTV recordings. The audio tracks are also generated and may have different acoustic properties from real surveillance environments.

\textbf{External validity.} The dataset of 30 scenes is substantially larger than the 8 scenes used in preliminary evaluations of prior work, but it remains limited. The fixed camera angle, single-scene design, and synthetic nature of the footage mean that the absolute F1 values should not be interpreted as deployment-ready accuracy.

\textbf{Construct validity.} The evaluation uses F1 score based on binary clip-level verdicts, following the paradigm established by the TRECVID Multimedia Event Detection (MED) task \citep{over2014trecvid}. Each scene is treated as a single clip: the ISEQL query either returns a result (the event is present) or does not (the event is absent), and the ground truth provides a binary positive/negative label per scene. This approach measures whether the system correctly identifies that a complex event occurs anywhere in a clip, but it does not capture temporal precision at the frame level. Interval-level overlap against ground truth is not applied because the events are compositional: they involve multiple intervals across multiple modalities (e.g., a vehicle escape spans running, entering a vehicle, engine sound, and tire squeal), and the ground truth does not define a single composite event interval to match against.

\textbf{Conclusion validity.} The effect sizes are large: re-ID raises visual F1 from 0.706 to 0.847 for the best model; Qwen2-Audio outperforms PANNs by 0.359 micro F1 points; union adds 0.091 F1 points over the best unimodal condition (visual F1 = 0.847 to multimodal F1 = 0.938). These magnitudes are large enough that statistical significance is not in question, even with 30 scenes.

\section{Key Contributions}
\label{sec:disc-contrib}

This thesis makes seven contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{First LALM for surveillance forensics.} Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct achieves perfect precision (1.000) and micro F1 = 0.788, substantially outperforming PANNs CNN14 (F1 = 0.429).
  \item \textbf{Object re-identification for VLM-based surveillance.} A two-phase tracking prompt with block-overlap matching unlocks the tracking-dependent events (loitering, vehicle escape, handoff) that per-frame ID assignment misses: e.g.\ Gemini 3.6 Flash loitering 0/5 to 5/5, raising visual F1 from 0.706 to 0.847.
  \item \textbf{First labeled multimodal forensic dataset.} 30 scenes, 6 event types, per-frame visual + audio + verdict ground truth.
  \item \textbf{Union-based multimodal design.} $C = A \cup B$ preserves all detections while temporal joins reduce recall.
  \item \textbf{VLM benchmark across four models.} Gemini 3.6 Flash is the best performer (F1 = 0.847; 30/30 TP when combined with Qwen2-Audio).
  \item \textbf{Closed-vocabulary LALM prompt.} Forces the LALM to select from predetermined sound classes, eliminating post-hoc normalisation.
  \item \textbf{Auditable forensic pipeline.} Every detection is the output of a readable, editable SQL query ,  no black-box reasoning.
\end{enumerate}
