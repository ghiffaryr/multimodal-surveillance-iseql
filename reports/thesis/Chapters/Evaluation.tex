\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{Evaluation}

This chapter reports the experimental results, structured by the three research questions defined in Chapter~\ref{Introduction}. Section~\ref{sec:eval-setup} describes the experimental setup. Section~\ref{sec:rq1} evaluates the impact of object re-identification (RQ1). Section~\ref{sec:rq2} compares the audio models across window and hop configurations (RQ2). Section~\ref{sec:rq3} evaluates the multimodal union condition against both unimodal baselines (RQ3). Section~\ref{sec:eval-summary} summarises the findings.

\section{Evaluation Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

The evaluation uses a dataset of 30 synthetic surveillance scenes (10 s, 1280$\times$720, 24 fps) generated with Dreamina Seedance. Each scene is annotated with per-frame ground truth for visual relations and audio classes, and event-level binary verdicts. The 30 scenes span 6 event types with 5 positive instances each.

Four VLM models were evaluated on condition A: Pixtral 12B, Ministral 3-14B, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and Gemini 3.6 Flash. Two audio providers were evaluated on condition B: PANNs CNN14 and Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, each across 8 window/hop configurations. Condition C combined every VLM with every audio provider via union. A re-identification ablation was conducted for each VLM by running without the tracking prompt.

The primary metric is the F1 score (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}), reported alongside precision, recall, and per-event TP/FP/FN counts.

Re-identification consistently improves recall by recovering the tracking-dependent events. For Gemini 3.6 Flash, recall rises from 0.467 to 0.900, driven by suspicious near vehicle (0/5 to 5/5), vehicle escape (0/5 to 3/5), and handoff (0/5 to 5/5). Precision evolves unevenly because re-ID both recovers previously missed tracking-dependent detections (raising recall) and adds a handful of false positives: it rises for Ministral 3-14B (+0.075) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (+0.086) but falls slightly for Gemini 3.6 Flash ($-$0.062) and Pixtral 12B ($-$0.061); false positives grow for all four models (e.g.\ Gemini 3.6 Flash 1 to 4, Ministral 3-14B 6 to 7). Re-ID raises true positives in every model (e.g.\ Gemini 3.6 Flash 14 to 27). The effect is concentrated on events requiring persistent object identity; short-duration events such as fight and vehicle collision are largely unaffected in all four models.

Several patterns emerge from the ablation. First, Qwen2-Audio achieves \textbf{perfect precision} (1.000) at the 5.0 s windows (5.0 s / 2.5 s and 5.0 s / 5.0 s): every detection is correct at these configurations. Its best micro F1 (0.788) occurs at (5.0 s, 2.5 s), with the 2.5 s / 1.25 s configuration close behind (0.687). Smaller windows (1.0 s) degrade precision because the model has insufficient acoustic context (0.385 at 1.0 s / 1.0 s); larger windows (10.0 s) also reduce precision to 0.800 and miss short-duration events. Second, PANNs CNN14 achieves its best F1 of 0.400 at (5.0 s, 5.0 s) but never exceeds this value. PANNs performs well on gunshot detection (high recall) but suffers from false positives and low recall on other events. The fight audio query uses a BEFORE($\delta:5$~s) operator; it did not improve recall because Qwen2-Audio reliably detects the shout but rarely emits the impact interval (impact recall 0.00--0.25 across configurations), and in the one scene where impact is detected it precedes the shout. The 2.5 s / 1.25 s configuration balances context size and temporal resolution and is used in the multimodal experiments; at this configuration Qwen2-Audio reaches F1 = 0.687.

\subsection{Thesis Claim Verification}

The claim holds on all 30 scenes: condition C is never worse than $\max(A, B)$. The union is most beneficial when the modalities are complementary: audio contributes additional true positives on vehicle escape that visual alone would have missed (visual 3/5, audio 5/5, union 5/5), while visual carries the fight detection (5/5) that audio completely misses. The LALM detects all 5 gunshot/explosion events through synonym resolution, adding one detection over visual alone. The two visual-only events (handoff, suspicious near vehicle) are unchanged in C since they have no audio query; handoff is recovered to 5/5 by re-identification.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:eval-summary}

The evaluation establishes three findings. \textbf{RQ1:} Object re-identification is essential for tracking-dependent events; without it, suspicious near vehicle, vehicle escape, and handoff all fail (0/5 for every model), because per-frame ID assignment fragments their intervals into single frames (e.g. Gemini 3.6 Flash suspicious near vehicle 0/5 to 5/5, escape 0/5 to 3/5, handoff 0/5 to 5/5). \textbf{RQ2:} Qwen2-Audio achieves perfect precision (1.000) at 2.5 s and 5.0 s windows and a micro F1 of 0.788 at 5.0 s / 2.5 s (0.687 at 2.5 s / 1.25 s), outperforming PANNs (best F1 = 0.400) at seven of the eight configurations. \textbf{RQ3:} The multimodal union achieves F1 = 0.938 with the best combination (Gemini 3.6 Flash + Qwen2-Audio, 30/30 TP), outperforming both unimodal baselines (visual F1 = 0.885, audio F1 = 0.788), and the claim $C \ge \max(A, B)$ holds on all 30 scenes.
